# Insights & Recommendations

## Key Insights

1. **Revenue grew, then plateaued.** Monthly revenue grew from $111,798 (Jan 2017) to a peak of $987,752 (Nov 2017, Black Friday), then stalled through mid-2018, ending around $838,568 in Aug 2018.

2. **Revenue is not category-concentrated.** Across 72 product categories, the top 3 account for just 25.8% of revenue and the top 10 for 62.4% — no single category drives the business.

3. **Only 3% of customers ever return.** Of 93,358 unique customers, just 2,801 placed a second order — growth depends almost entirely on new-customer acquisition, not retention.

4. **Late deliveries badly hurt satisfaction.** Orders delivered late average a 2.57-star review vs 4.29 stars on time — a 1.72-point gap, roughly 40% lower. 8% of delivered orders (7,661) were late.

5. **Spend is concentrated in a small top group.** The top 10% of customers (9,336 people) account for 38.3% of total spend, while the bottom 50% contribute only ~18.4% combined.

6. **Seller revenue is concentrated in São Paulo.** 9 of the top 10 sellers by revenue are based in SP.

7. **32.6% of customers spend above the platform average** — confirming the right-skewed distribution seen in the decile breakdown.

8. **SP's delivery reliability doesn't match its revenue dominance.** SP has an 8.5% late-delivery rate, worse than 9 of the other 12 states with meaningful volume, despite carrying ~82% of all order volume.

## How These Connect

Revenue growth (Insight 1) depended on constant new-customer acquisition, since retention is only 3% (Insight 3). Category concentration isn't the constraint (Insight 2), but delivery reliability is a concrete, fixable lever (Insight 4) — and it's concentrated exactly where the business has the most volume and seller activity: São Paulo (Insights 6 & 8). Meanwhile, a small high-value customer segment already exists and could be protected and grown (Insight 5).

## Recommendations

**1. Fix delivery reliability in São Paulo first.**
SP carries ~82% of order volume and an 8.5% late rate, worse than most other states. A capacity or logistics-partner improvement here affects the most orders per unit of effort.
*Metric to watch: late-delivery % in SP, tracked monthly.*

**2. Build a retention offer for first-time buyers.**
With a 3% repeat rate, even a small lift compounds — moving to 5% adds ~1,900 repeat customers. Target the 30–60 day window after a first delivered order.
*Metric to watch: repeat purchase rate (Query 3 methodology).*

**3. Create a top-decile customer program.**
The top 10% of customers already drive 38.3% of spend. A loyalty tier protects and grows the segment the business is most exposed to losing.
*Metric to watch: decile-1 spend share, tracked quarterly.*
